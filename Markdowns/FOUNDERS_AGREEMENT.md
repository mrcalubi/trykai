## What this document is, and is not

This is a record of what the three of us have agreed. It is not a share certificate and carries no legal force over ownership, because TryKai is currently a sole proprietorship and a sole proprietorship cannot issue shares. Legally, TryKai is owned entirely by Caleb, who carries all its liabilities personally.

What this document does is establish, in writing and signed, the basis on which a cap table will be constructed if and when TryKai converts to a private limited company. It exists so that nobody is relying on memory or goodwill about what was agreed, and so that a future conversion is a formality rather than a negotiation.

### Vesting
Four years, with a one year cliff, applying to all three founders including Caleb.

- No equity is earned on departure before twelve months of continuous contribution
- After the cliff, equity vests monthly across the remaining 36 months
- Ruiheng's vesting clock started July 2026

Caleb vests his own equity on the same terms. This is deliberate: it signals good faith and protects the business if any founder departs.

### Right of first refusal
If any founder wishes to sell or transfer vested equity, the remaining founders have the right to purchase that stake at fair value before it may be offered to any outside party.

### Refresh triggers
Equity is fixed at the split above. It may be renegotiated at four defined moments only, never on a rolling or automatic basis.

1. **Platform launch** — live, payments working, 15+ listings, first booking completed by a stranger
2. **Six months post launch**
3. **Proof of concept** — three consecutive months of booking growth without proportionally more founder effort
4. **Extraordinary contribution** — any founder does something materially outside their defined role that changes the trajectory of the business. All three must agree it qualifies before the conversation begins.

Refresh conversations are evidenced by the monthly contribution log, not by recollection.

## 2. Profit sharing

**Distributed profit is split three ways equally.**

This diverges from the equity split deliberately. Equity reflects ownership, long term risk, and legal liability. Profit share reflects who is doing the work now. All three founders are actively contributing, so distributed cash is shared equally.

### Reinvestment
**50 per cent of net profit is reinvested in the business. 50 per cent is available for distribution.**

### Distribution floor
No profit is distributed until **both** conditions are satisfied:

1. The founder loan is repaid in full, and
2. Net profit has averaged **S$500 per month or more across three consecutive months**

The three month average prevents a single strong month triggering a distribution that a weak following month reverses. Below this threshold, all profit is reinvested, on the basis that trivial distributions are worth less than the growth they would fund.

### Salaries
No founder draws a salary during the pre revenue and early revenue phases. Once revenue consistently exceeds operating costs, a modest stipend may be discussed, funded from revenue rather than personal savings.

## 6. Accountability and removal

**Monthly check in**, all three founders, 30 minutes, at the start of each month. Each person reports against the previous month's stated deliverables.

**Contribution log**, updated monthly by each founder, forming the evidence base for any equity refresh discussion.

**Where a founder is not contributing:**
- *Trigger*: two consecutive missed monthly deliverables, or two or more weeks absent without communication
- *Process*: one deliberate conversation, not a casual mention, in which the issue is named clearly and a 30 day improvement plan with specific measurable targets is agreed
- *Escalation*: if the plan is not met, a 2/3 majority vote may formally remove that person from their role
- Removal from a role does not automatically affect already vested equity. Vested equity is governed by section 1.

---

## 7. Amendment

This agreement is amended when something actually changes, not on a fixed schedule. The equity refresh triggers in section 1 define when equity may be renegotiated. Any other change requires 2/3 agreement.

Each amendment produces a new version, dated and signed by all three founders. Superseded versions are retained rather than deleted, so the history of what was agreed and when remains visible.

At each monthly check in, one question is asked: **does this agreement still match reality?** If yes, nothing happens. If no, it is amended and re signed.

## 8. Conversion to a private limited company

On conversion to a Pte Ltd, this agreement forms the basis of the cap table. Conversion is planned once traction is demonstrated, and should be planned alongside cap table finalisation rather than after it. Professional legal advice is to be taken for the conversion itself.
