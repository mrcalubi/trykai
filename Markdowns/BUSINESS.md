Positioning, product structure, competition, business model, growth, team, and legal entity. This is the single source of truth for anything commercial or structural. Product and technical decisions live in DECISIONS.md; execution lives in OPERATIONS.md.

## 1. What TryKai is

A peer to peer skill and experience marketplace built for Singapore. People with a skill or hobby list short, in person sessions teaching it. Local guests browse, book, and pay through the platform. TryKai earns a booking fee.

Sessions are priced between S$10 and S$40 per person, an order of magnitude below a comparable studio workshop, and require no course commitment.

**Target audience**
- Primary: 18 to 28, university students and young working adults
- Secondary: 28 to 35, young professionals with more disposable income
- Hosts: typically 20 to 35
- Minimum age 18, enforced in the terms. Minors create contract and safeguarding complications we are not equipped for.

We serve locals, not tourists. This is a deliberate difference from every large experience platform operating here.

## 2. The two lane model

Two distinct lanes on one platform, sharing the same backend, accounts, and trust infrastructure, but presented separately so guests self select into the right expectations before browsing rather than discovering a mismatch mid scroll.

Both lanes exist because the founding use cases do not collapse into one buyer intent. Latte art and DJ-ing are novelty, capped at two or three sessions. Learning Japanese is genuine multi session progression. Forcing everything into one framing either abandons the original insight or abandons the better repeat economics.

### Lane 1: Try Something New
*Launches first.*

The founding insight: Singapore is perceived as expensive and boring, and there is real appetite for affordable, low commitment local activity, including date ideas, group hangouts, and solo novelty seeking.

- **Sessions:** one to three, capped naturally by intent
- **Buyer mindset:** impulsive, social, novelty seeking
- **Examples:** latte art, DJ-ing basics, pottery taster, casual cooking, beginner fitness
- **Trust bar:** ID verification is sufficient. Worst case is a mediocre afternoon.
- **Monetisation:** per booking fee. No bundles needed at this session count.
- **Supply:** easiest to recruit. Most people have some casual skill worth sharing once or twice.
- **Launch categories:** two or three deep, not thin across all six

### Lane 2: Build a Skill
*Launches second, deliberately slower.*

- **Sessions:** four to fifteen or more, via bundles or course structures
- **Buyer mindset:** outcome oriented, price and results sensitive. Compares TryKai against tutors, language schools, and Skillshare, not against "things to do this weekend".
- **Examples:** language, instrument, technique focused coaching, resume relevant upskilling
- **Trust bar:** higher and different in kind. The guest's fear is not "is this person a scammer", it is "is this person actually good enough to teach me something I am relying on". Requires a separate Skill-verified badge layered on ID verification, plus outcome oriented review prompts.
- **Monetisation:** credit bundles and multi session courses become essential rather than optional
- **Supply:** needs credentialed peers or small businesses. Quality bar matters more than speed; rushing this lane damages the trust tier it depends on.
- **Launch trigger:** two or three genuinely credible, skill verifiable hosts confirmed in one category, likely Language

**The resume and upskilling angle** is the strongest demand signal in Lane 2. Career anxiety and job market pressure make that motivation durable in a way novelty is not. Worth leaning into deliberately, competing on price and on real person accountability against faceless online courses.

### The direct peer competitor: ToGatherSG

*Observed 23 August 2026. Website: togathersg.com. iOS apps live June 2026. Building since October 2025.*

**Positioning:** "Singapore's first marketplace for home-based experiences." Same core idea as TryKai, similar target market, launched a few months earlier.

**Current state after roughly two months live:**
- 22 experiences live
- 5 total reviews across all 22 experiences, concentrated on two listings (a mosaic workshop and a matcha and crafts session)
- Price spread S$15 to S$125 for a two hour session, wide fluctuation with no clear anchor band
- Session model appears to depend on group minimums, some listings state "cancelled and refunded if minimum not reached"

**What the head start has and has not produced.** Eight months of build plus two to three months live has produced supply but essentially no reputation, five reviews across twenty two listings. The market is not locked up. TryKai is not late to this, TryKai is early to the part that actually compounds, which is reviews and trust.

#### Weaknesses to exploit

1. **App download required to browse.** The website's sole call to action is "Download Now." No listings are visible without installing. For an "I'm bored on Friday" use case, that install step is a real drop off. TryKai runs in a browser, someone can share a listing link and the recipient sees it instantly with no install and no account. Worth stating explicitly in messaging: browse now, no app needed.

2. **Two separate apps, one for guests and one for hosts.** A would be host has to discover, download, and set up a second app before they can even fill in the onboarding form. That is a hurdle at the top of the recruitment funnel, exactly where friction hurts most. TryKai's unified account, guest by default, becomes a host on first listing, is the correct answer and matches how Airbnb, Carousell, and every serious marketplace works. Do not deviate from this.

3. **No romantic or occasion framing anywhere.** Zero mention of couples, dates, or occasions across the app or the website after eight months of building. This is real world validation that Date Mode is genuinely unclaimed territory, not a crowded angle. Worth leaning into more explicitly at launch than the current soft "solo, with friends, or on a date" subhead does.

4. **Group minimum session model.** Listings that require a minimum guest count to run mean a guest can book, feel committed, and have the experience cancelled because strangers did not fill it. TryKai's smaller session model does not have this failure mode. Worth stating implicitly as a guarantee: your booking is not contingent on strangers.

5. **Muddied target audience.** The app does not produce a strong "I want to try this" reaction. No clear guest archetype, no clear use case anchor. TryKai's positioning around identity building, date planning, and the cinema ticket price point is sharper.

6. **Price spread is chaotic.** Session prices range from S$15 to S$125 with no discernible anchor band, which reads as an aggregator rather than a curated marketplace. TryKai's disciplined S$10 to S$40 band is a positioning asset, not just a pricing preference. Do not drift out of it under supply side pressure.

#### What to respect, not imitate

**Their design is polished, and it is polished for a different audience than ours.** Cartoon mascots, comic style banners, bright purple. That is a considered aesthetic choice for a casual, gamified feel, and it is literally the exact direction DESIGN.md considered and rejected on the grounds that comic and bubble undermines trust in a platform handling real money and real strangers meeting up. TryKai's navy and cream, warm but credible palette is the more disciplined choice for the actual positioning, and should not be softened to look more like theirs.

**Their host onboarding flow is decent as a form.** Photo, address, phone, categories, optional social, optional food safety, submit for review. The flow itself is coherent. What is wrong is that it lives in a separate app. When TryKai builds equivalent host onboarding, it should match this level of structure while staying inside one unified account.

#### One small UX tell worth noting

**"Delete my account" appears on the same screen as "Submit for review."** A destructive action on the same surface as a happy path action is the kind of thing a design review catches. Minor on its own, but evidence the app's UX is shakier than the visual surface suggests. Reinforces that "looks beautiful" is not the same as "is well designed," and imitating the surface would import the underlying weaknesses too.

#### One caution before betting the strategy on this analysis

The observations above are from a single browsing session on 23 August 2026. Reviewing them again in a month or two makes sense, since:
- 22 live listings is a real supply base, they can plausibly reach 50+ by TryKai's launch
- Their review count is thin now but could grow quickly if they hit an inflection
- Two separate apps could be consolidated into one at any point

Nothing here suggests they are a serious existential threat today. It does suggest the market is genuinely open, and it validates several of TryKai's already made decisions rather than requiring new ones.

**The honest competitive risk is not another platform.** It is inertia, and free alternatives: existing friend groups, hawker centres, parks. Our answer is price, low commitment, and making the first booking easy.

## 3a. What the edge actually is

Strip away the features and one thing is genuinely hard to copy: **supply that would not list anywhere else.** Nobody else has a particular person teaching ring making for S$25.

The sharper version is that the price point and the supply are the same fact. A pottery studio cannot profitably run a S$20 class; rent, staff, insurance, and utilities set a floor somewhere around S$50 to S$60. Someone in their own kitchen with their own equipment has no fixed cost, so S$25 works. **The S$10 to S$40 band is not a pricing preference, it is a band that only zero fixed cost supply can occupy.**

That is why the gap exists between free things and expensive things. Not because nobody noticed it, but because the economics exclude every incumbent from serving it.

The plain sentence for the market: **learn something real from someone who actually does it, for the price of a cinema ticket.**

**The honest weakness:** none of this is defensible through technology. It is defensible because recruiting individuals one at a time is slow, high touch work that does not accelerate with funding. That is a real moat against a better funded competitor, and it is also why this will feel like a grind.

### The date use case, and the price that proves it

The comparison set is not other classes. It is what a couple would otherwise spend on a Saturday.

| Alternative | Cost for two |
|---|---|
| Cinema | ~S$30 |
| Cat cafe | ~S$30 |
| Bowling | ~S$35 |
| Casual dinner | S$40 to S$60 |
| Escape room | S$60 to S$80 |
| Cocktail bar | S$60 to S$80 |

At S$15 per person two people pay about S$33 all in, which is a cinema ticket except they talk to each other and leave with something they made. At S$25 each it is about S$54, which is dinner, and dinner is a strong incumbent. At S$35 each it is S$75 and the cheap date framing is gone.

**The validating band is S$15 to S$25 per person.** Under S$60 for two is where cheap date is literally true.

Message accordingly: anchor against the alternative, not against other classes. A comparison does more work than any adjective.

### Host economics, which determine whether supply stays

A S$25 session, four guests, 90 minutes plus 30 minutes of setup and clearing, about S$8 per head in materials:

| Guests | Gross | After materials | Per hour |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 100 | 68 | **34** |
| 3 | 75 | 51 | 25 |
| 2 | 50 | 34 | 17 |
| 1 | 25 | 17 | 8 |

At full capacity that is comparable to private tutoring and roughly triple a retail shift. At two guests it is marginal. At one it is a hobby.

**Fill rate is worth about four times what any platform fee costs a host.** A 10 per cent host fee on a full session takes them from S$34 to S$29 an hour. Going from two guests to four takes them from S$17 to S$34. That is the honest answer to a host who objects to the fee, and it is also the honest test of whether the fee is deserved: if TryKai is not filling sessions, it has not earned it.

## 4. Designing for Singapore

Working assumption: Singaporean users skew toward lower inclination to seek out novel stranger contact than markets where "meet new people" apps thrive. Structural responses:

- **Lead with the activity, not the social angle.** The pitch is "learn pottery", not "meet new people". Social contact is a byproduct, not the ask.
- **A recurring host pays the stranger discomfort cost once, not repeatedly.** A one off marketplace demands a fresh "meet a stranger" decision every booking.
- **Default to small group or one to one** over large mixers.
- **Trust signals carry more weight here.** ID verification, reviews, and delayed address disclosure should be sold as a feature, not treated as background compliance.

## 5. Human desire and what actually sustains this

Most durable consumer products tap chronic desires that do not resolve on use. TryKai's core challenge is that **novelty, Lane 1's primary draw, does resolve.** Try latte art, curiosity satisfied, move on. That is precisely why one off experience marketplaces struggle.

The desires that do not self extinguish:

**Identity and status.** The strongest available. The 18 to 28 demographic is actively constructing a self image and cares about being seen as interesting, skilled, and intentional. "I'm learning Japanese" is an identity claim, not a hobby. People pay to become the kind of person who does things.

*Implication: sell identity, not activities. Not "try latte art" but "become someone who makes great coffee".*

**Social performance.** Struggle and visible effort carry the strongest social currency, which is why Strava works. Lane 1 signals taste and lifestyle, weaker but real. Lane 2 signals discipline and effort, stronger, because the audience knows it cost something. Do not flatten the two into the same achievement framing.

**Romantic and occasion driven planning.** The "great date planner" identity refuels itself with every new relationship, anniversary, and occasion. Date Mode taps this at near zero cost.

**Sunk cost and progress investment.** Once someone is three sessions into Japanese, stopping feels like abandoning something. Bundles and stated goals work with this psychology.

**The framing:** TryKai is not a booking platform. It is where you become a more interesting, more capable version of yourself. The booking is the input. The identity is the product.

## 6. Business model

| Stage | Trigger | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Stage 1 | Launch, supply-building | Guest booking fee. Host fee waived for founding hosts permanently; other hosts get three free bookings then 10%. |
| Stage 2 | Repeat behaviour, 500+ bookings historically used as the volume marker | Credit bundles, multi session courses, group discounts. Host fee already running per host, not waiting on a platform-wide count. |
| Stage 3 | 2,000+ bookings | Host subscription tier, around S$29/month. Primary long term margin driver. |

**Wants versus needs.** Pure needs categories, tutoring and certifications, are dominated by entrenched incumbents. Target the overlap instead: things that feel like wants but function like needs. Resume relevant upskilling is the clearest example.

## 7. Growth philosophy

**Core constraint: acquisition must get cheaper over time, not more expensive.** Singapore consumer CAC of S$5 to S$20+ exceeds our margin on a single booking, so paid acquisition does not pencil out until repeat economics are proven.

**Compounding mechanisms, in priority order**
1. **Referral loops.** Credit for both parties when a referred user completes a first booking.
2. **Review and trust compounding.** Every completed, reviewed booking reduces friction for the next guest.
3. **Host driven promotion.** Hosts earning real income promote their own listings through their own channels. Supply side marketing that scales with their motivation, at no platform cost.
4. **Category density.** Deep coverage in two or three categories creates a "this is alive" feeling. Thin coverage everywhere reads as empty.

### Evidence gated spending
Both founders will spend hundreds personally given reasonable confidence of thousands back. Not unlimited risk, not zero.

1. Prove unit economics manually first, at zero cost, during the organic launch phase.
2. Only spend once the numbers are known. Size any test, S$200 to S$500, against a calculated return from step 1, not optimism.
3. Spend in small, capped, reversible tranches. Never continue an underperforming channel on sunk cost reasoning.
4. Once a channel is proven, fund further spend from revenue, not fresh personal savings.

### What enough progress looks like
Deliberately underestimated, so that motivation dips do not trigger premature strategy changes.

- **Month 1 to 2:** live, 15 to 20 listings, single digit completed bookings. Success is the mechanism working end to end, a stranger pays, shows up, leaves a review. Not volume.
- **Month 3 to 4:** low double digit bookings. At least one organic repeat or referral without founder prompting.
- **Month 5 to 6:** bookings trending up month on month without proportionally more founder effort. This is the real checkpoint.
- **Failure signal:** not slow absolute numbers, which we expect, but a flat or declining trend, or growth that only happens when founders push harder each time.

## 8. Launch strategy

- **Lane sequenced, not simultaneous.** Lane 1 first. Lane 2 soft launched later, starting with one strong category once two or three credible hosts are confirmed.
- **Category concentrated within Lane 1.** Deep in two or three categories, not thin across six.
- **Geography is secondary.** Singapore is compact with good transit; category depth matters more than hyperlocal clustering.
- **Mixed sourcing.** Warm contacts first, then small business outreach framed as "bookings you would not otherwise get, free to find out if it works".
- **Risk to monitor:** if business supply dominates because it is easier to recruit at volume while peer supply lags, TryKai drifts toward a Klook style aggregator identity. That should be a deliberate choice if it happens, not an accident of which channel was easier.

### Ownership

**Vesting:** four years, one year cliff, all three founders including Caleb. Vesting own equity signals good faith and protects the company if anyone departs. No equity earned on departure before twelve months. Post cliff, monthly over the remaining 36 months. Ruiheng's clock started July 2026.

**Right of first refusal:** remaining founders may buy a departing founder's vested stake at fair value before it can be transferred to any outside party.

**Reserve pool:** not carved out. Revisit before adding contributors, so dilution is planned rather than entirely founder funded by accident.

### Equity refresh triggers
Fixed at the above split, renegotiable at four defined moments only, not on a rolling basis.

1. **Platform launch.** Live at trykai.sg, payments working, 15+ listings, first booking by a stranger. Assess technical and host recruitment delivery against defined roles.
2. **Six months post launch.** Review against monthly deliverable logs. Has load drifted unevenly?
3. **Proof of concept.** Three consecutive months of booking growth without proportionally more founder effort. The most significant refresh point. Ruiheng's 10 per cent is the most likely number to move upward if technical delivery has been the engine.
4. **Extraordinary contribution.** Any founder does something materially outside their role that changes the trajectory. All three must agree it qualifies before the conversation starts.

### Profit sharing
**Three ways equal: Caleb, Aakash, Ruiheng.**

This diverges deliberately from the 70/20/10 equity split, and the divergence is the point. **Equity tracks ownership, long term risk, and legal liability. Profit share tracks who is doing the work right now.** Ruiheng is the sole engineer carrying the critical path at 8 hours a week; an equal share of distributed cash reflects that, while equity continues to reflect what each person has put at risk.

*Changed 16 August 2026. Ruiheng was previously excluded from profit sharing entirely.*

### Reinvestment and the distribution floor
**Flat 50 per cent reinvested, 50 per cent distributed.** No phase table. Simplicity was chosen over precision here on purpose.

**Nothing is distributed until both conditions are met:**
1. The founder loan is fully repaid, and
2. Net profit averages **S$500 per month or more across three consecutive months**

The rolling average matters: one strong December should not trigger a distribution that January reverses.

At exactly the floor, the distributed half is S$250, or about S$83 each. Small, but it is the point at which the accounting stops being pointless. Below that threshold every dollar is more useful funding the next growth step.

**No salaries** during pre revenue and early revenue. Once revenue consistently exceeds operating costs, a modest founder stipend can be discussed, funded from revenue rather than savings.
